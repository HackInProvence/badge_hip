The Deep-Sea Cicada
The cicadas of Hack In Provence

== 1
The harbour of La Ciotat is waking up. The tall shipyard cranes stand against a pink sky, and the water smells of salt.

You are Cicadina, a young and very curious cicada, and today is your first scientific dive!

At the end of the quay waits a small yellow submarine, the Little Nautilus. On its roof, Professor Hippolyta, a seahorse with glasses, waves a fin.

-> 2 : Climb aboard right now
-> 3 : Visit the shop first
-> 4 : Talk to the gull on the quay

== 2
"Welcome aboard!" says Hippolyta. "Our mission: find the cable station."

In 1871, a telegraph cable ran from this coast to Algiers. A small relay station on the seabed repeated its messages. Then everybody forgot about it.

"If we find it, we can plug in a hydrophone and listen to the sea. Where is it? Painted on an old buoy, in code."

-> 5 : Cast off!

== 3
The harbour shop smells of rope and varnish. Behind the counter, Madam Mussel, a very old mussel, opens her shell a crack.

"A diving cicada! For an explorer, it's free. But only one thing, mind you."

On the counter: a waterproof signal lamp and a tin of sardines.

-> 6 : Take the signal lamp
-> 7 : Take the sardines
-> 2 : Nothing, thanks: all aboard!

== 4
On a mooring post sits a huge gull with a pirate patch over one eye. It's Gaston, the treasure-hunting seagull.

"Psst, cicada. A submarine, a mysterious look... You're after a treasure, aren't you?"

-> 8 : "No, an old cable."
-> 9 : "Yes! A chest full of gold!"
-> 2 : Say hello and walk on

== 5
The Little Nautilus slides out of the harbour. You pass under the big shipyard crane, then by the Green Island.

Three dolphins leap next to the porthole, as if to wish you a good trip.

Far away, a rusty buoy bobs on the waves. Behind it, the sky is turning grey.

-> 10 : Head for the buoy
-> 26 : Follow the dolphins

== 6
Madam Mussel hands you the lamp: a big yellow box with a trigger.

"Short press, long press: that's Morse code. Sailors use it when the radio goes quiet."

You clip it to your belt.

+[lamp]

-> 2 : Hurry to the submarine
-> 4 : Go and see the gull

== 7
A tin of sardines in olive oil, with a smiling sardine on the lid.

"You never know," says Madam Mussel. "At the bottom of the sea, everybody's hungry."

You slip it into your bag.

+[sardines]

-> 2 : Hurry to the submarine
-> 4 : Go and see the gull

== 8
"A cable?" Gaston pulls a face. "Copper, wires, pfff. Not one gold coin."

He rummages under his wing and pulls out a yellowed paper.

"Here, found it in a bottle. Just drawings of cables. Boring."

An old sea chart! A cross marks a "chimney" on the roof of the station.

+[chart]

-> 2 : Thank him and go aboard

== 9
Gaston's eye lights up like a lighthouse.

"Gold! I knew it! I'm coming with you, partner. Fifty-fifty, right?"

He lands on the roof of the submarine and refuses to move. You feel a bit guilty: that wasn't exactly true.

+[Gaston]

-> 2 : Go aboard, Gaston on the roof

== 10
The buoy is covered in rust and tiny crabs. On its side, white painted letters:

FDEOH DW WKH FUDQH

And below, smaller: "Jules C. moved forward 3."

"Julius Caesar!" says Hippolyta. "He shifted every letter along the alphabet. To read it, go back 3: F becomes C..."

-> 11 : "CABLE AT THE CRANE"
-> 12 : "SOUND OF THE WAVES"
-> 14 [Gaston] : Let Gaston read it
-> 13 : No time, dive!

== 11
F, E, D... C! D, C, B... A! Letter by letter, you read:

CABLE AT THE CRANE

"Well done!" Hippolyta does a happy somersault. "An old shipyard crane fell into the sea during a storm. The cable must pass close by!"

+[code]

-> 15 : Go on

== 12
Hippolyta checks her notebook.

"Hmm. S moved forward 3 gives V, not F. That doesn't fit."

You count again on your legs. She's right: start from the letters on the buoy and go back three.

-> 10 : Read the buoy again
-> 13 : Never mind, dive without it

== 13
"All right," sighs Hippolyta. "We'll follow the cable along the bottom. It will just take longer."

She closes the hatch. At that very moment, a fat raindrop splashes on the porthole.

-> 15 : Go on

== 14
Gaston leans over, squints with his one eye and declares:

"Easy. It says: TREASURE HERE."

He dives head first along the buoy's chain... and comes up with an old boot full of seaweed.

"The treasure is in the boot. Obviously."

Hippolyta rolls her eyes.

-> 10 : Decipher it yourself

== 15
The wind rises all at once. The mistral! The waves grow, the foam flies, the Little Nautilus bobs like a cork.

"Emergency dive!" shouts Hippolyta. "Hold on!"

She pulls the lever. Everything depends on the next wave...

-> 16 [de 1-2] : A huge wave is coming...
-> 17 [de 3-6] : The submarine dives in time

== 16
The wave lifts the submarine, turns it half over, drops it again. You roll from the ceiling to the floor. So does your stomach, the other way round.

"Everything all right?" asks Hippolyta, looking very green.

Outside, the storm howls.

-> 17 : Dive anyway!
-> 18 : Go back to the harbour

== 17
Ten metres, twenty metres... The noise of the storm fades away. Under the waves, everything is calm, blue and silent.

A school of salema fish glitters past. On the sand, a long black pipe covered in shells stretches away: the cable!

"Which way?" asks Hippolyta.

-> 19 [code] : Head for the sunken crane
-> 20 : Follow the cable
-> 21 : Visit the seagrass meadow

== 18 FIN perdu
You go back to the harbour, soaked to the antennae. Madam Mussel serves you a hot panisse while the rain drums on the roof.

The storm lasts three days. On the fourth, Hippolyta has to give the submarine back to the lab.

The station will wait: it's used to it, after a hundred years.

Moral: under the storm, the sea is calm. You just had to dive.

== 19
A giant shadow appears: the sunken crane! Lying on the seabed, its steel arm has become a forest of orange sponges and red sea fans.

Hundreds of fish live there. The cable passes under the crane's cabin... but the way is blocked by a huge brown lump that snores.

-> 30 : Get closer to the brown lump

== 20
The cable winds across the sand. You follow it for a long time, headlights on. A starfish waves at you with one arm.

The cable reaches a fork: to the left, a field of old amphoras; to the right, a big metal shadow.

-> 22 : Left, to the amphoras
-> 19 : Right, to the big shadow

== 21
A meadow under the sea! The posidonia waves like long green hair. "It's not seaweed," Hippolyta explains. "It's a flowering plant. It makes oxygen and shelters thousands of baby fish."

Suddenly, BOOM: a boat's anchor drops and drags through the meadow, tearing out whole clumps!

-> 23 [lamp] : Signal to the boat
-> 24 : Free the anchor with the claw
-> 25 : That's not our mission

== 22
The field of amphoras looks like an old sunken market. From inside one of them, two eyes are watching you.

An octopus slowly comes out, changing colour: white, red, striped, spotted.

"Hello! I'm Octave. I collect shiny things and I love puzzles. Want to play?"

-> 37 : Talk with Octave

== 23
You press the lamp against the roof porthole and flash: short, long, short...

Up on deck, a little girl reads the Morse code with her phone:

"ANCHOR ON SEAGRASS. PLEASE MOVE."

A few minutes later, the anchor goes up, and the boat moves over to a patch of sand.

-> 28 : Go on

== 24
Hippolyta extends the submarine's robotic claw. You steer with the joystick, tongue between your mandibles.

You must free the anchor without hurting the plants. It's tricky...

-> 28 [de 4-6] : Gently... got it?
-> 29 [de 1-3] : Oops, the claw is stuck...

== 25
"Not our mission?" Hippolyta looks at you over her glasses. "One metre of meadow takes a century to grow, Cicadina."

You blush to the tips of your antennae. Too late: the boat is already leaving, and there's a hole in the meadow.

A little sad, you set off along the cable again.

-> 22 : Follow the cable

== 26
The dolphins circle the submarine, laughing in clicks. They race west, come back, race off again: they want to play!

Hippolyta hesitates.

"It's wonderful... but the buoy is that way, and the sky is getting dark."

-> 10 : Go back to the buoy
-> 27 : Follow them a little longer

== 27 FIN perdu
A little longer, then a little longer... The dolphins lead you all the way to the Old Port of Marseille, their favourite place: the fish market.

The Little Nautilus has run out of battery. A water taxi tows you back to La Ciotat, while tourists take pictures.

Moral: dolphins are great guides, but never to where you're going.

== 28
Peace returns. A small cuttlefish swims up, changes colour to say thank you, then points somewhere with its tentacles.

"She says the octopus of the amphoras knows all about the cable," Hippolyta translates.

A ball of seagrass fibre rolls past the porthole. On the beaches, people call them sea balls.

-> 22 : Go and see the octopus

== 29 FIN perdu
The claw gets stuck in the anchor. Above, the boat pulls up its anchor... and the Little Nautilus with it!

There you are, dangling under the hull like a giant key ring. The skipper, a man in shorts, can't believe his eyes.

He kindly brings you back to the harbour, where the whole of La Ciotat comes to take pictures.

Moral: the meadow is saved, but the expedition ends here.

== 30
The brown lump opens one eye. It's a grouper, as big as a fridge, with an old sailor's moustache.

"More tourists! This is MY crane. The passage is closed."

He yawns: the Little Nautilus could almost fit in his mouth.

-> 31 [sardines] : Offer him the sardines
-> 33 [Octave] : Let Octave talk to him
-> 32 : Ask him politely
-> 22 : Try somewhere else

== 31
You open the tin in front of the porthole. The grouper sniffs. His moustache quivers.

"Olive oil? Hmph. Just this once."

He swallows the lot in one gulp, burps a huge bubble and moves aside.

"The station is behind me, in the crevice. And keep it quiet!"

-[sardines]

-> 47 : Enter the crevice

== 32
"Politely?" the grouper grumbles. "Answer my question and I'll move. Otherwise, I sleep."

He points a fin at the seagrass meadow in the distance.

"How old can a meadow like that one be?"

-> 35 : Older than the pyramids
-> 34 : A few months, like grass
-> 34 : None of your business!

== 33
Octave swims up to the grouper and pats him on the head.

"Marcel! These are my friends. And you owe me a game of draughts."

The grouper sighs a very, very long sigh.

"Fine, fine. For Octave. But keep it quiet."

He moves aside, revealing a dark crevice behind the crane.

-> 47 : Enter the crevice

== 34
The grouper closes his eyes.

"Pfff. Tourists. I'm sleeping."

And he snores, so loudly that the sea fans shake.

-> 36 : Wait for him to wake up
-> 22 : Look for help elsewhere

== 35
The grouper opens both eyes, surprised.

"Correct. Some meadows are thousands of years old. They were here before the harbour, before the boats, before the cicadas."

He slowly moves aside.

"You're smarter than the others. The station is in the crevice. Treat it with respect."

-> 47 : Enter the crevice

== 36 FIN perdu
You wait. The grouper snores. You wait some more. A grouper can live fifty years, and this one loves his naps.

In the evening, the battery light of the Little Nautilus blinks: time to go up.

Moral: a grouper only wakes up for a good answer... or a sardine.

== 37
Octave curls around an amphora.

"The cable? Of course I know it. And the station too: that's where I found my finest treasure."

He gives you a cheeky look.

"But nothing is free down here. A puzzle for my help!"

-> 38 : Accept the puzzle
-> 39 : "Just tell us the way..."

== 38
Octave holds shells in all eight arms.

"Listen carefully. This morning, I had 2 shells in each arm. A crab stole 3. How many do I have left?"

He changes colour, impatient.

-> 40 : Thirteen
-> 41 : Sixteen
-> 41 : Five

== 39
"Just the way?" Octave turns grey with disappointment. "Far too easy! Life without puzzles is like the sea without salt."

He crosses four arms over the other four and waits.

-> 38 : All right, the puzzle!

== 40
"Thirteen! Eight times two is sixteen, minus three!" Octave turns bright pink with joy.

He rummages in his amphora and pulls out a big brass key, all green with age.

"My finest treasure. It comes from the station. Take it!"

+[key]

-> 42 : "Come with us, Octave!"
-> 43 : Thank him and leave

== 41
"Wrong!" Octave laughs, sending bubbles everywhere. "But I'm a good sport: a rematch?"

He puts three shells on the sand, hides a pebble under one of them and shuffles them with all eight arms, very, very fast.

-> 44 : Play the rematch

== 42
Octave sticks to the porthole like a giant suction cup.

"With pleasure! I know the guard of the passage, Marcel the grouper. He's grumpy, but he's my draughts partner."

+[Octave]

-> 30 : Head for the sunken crane

== 43
"Good luck!" says Octave. "The station is behind the sunken crane. Watch out for the grouper, he's grumpy."

He vanishes into his amphora, leaving just one arm out to wave goodbye.

-> 30 : Head for the sunken crane

== 44
Eight arms, three shells: impossible to follow! You point at a shell at random...

-> 40 [de 4-6] : Octave lifts the shell...
-> 45 [de 1-3] : Octave lifts the shell...

== 45
Empty! The pebble was under another one.

"One more?" Octave suggests, eyes shining. "Octopuses never get tired of games."

Hippolyta looks at her watch.

-> 38 : Try the puzzle again
-> 46 : Play again, and again...
-> 30 : Leave without his help

== 46 FIN
You play. Shells, riddles, draughts, even Morse code with colours. Octave is delighted, and so are you.

When the battery runs low, Hippolyta takes you back to the harbour. The station will wait, but you are now the champion of octopus games.

Every summer, you come back to play with Octave. It wasn't the mission, but it's a fine friendship.

== 47
Behind the crane, a crevice plunges into darkness. The Little Nautilus is too big to get in.

"Your turn, Cicadina," says Hippolyta, handing you a bubble helmet. "You're small, you'll fit."

You leave through the airlock. The water is cool, your wings float behind you. Deep down, something gleams.

-> 48 [lamp] : Switch on the lamp
-> 49 [!lamp] : Feel your way forward

== 48
The yellow beam lights up the crevice. Shrimps flee, blinking. On the rock wall, an engraved plate:

"RELAY STATION - LA CIOTAT-ALGIERS CABLE - 1871"

In front of you, a metal dome covered in coral, with a round door.

-> 50 : Go up to the door

== 49
It's as dark as an inkwell. You switch on the LEDs of your badge: a tiny blue glow.

You bump into something soft.

"Excuse me!" says a sea cucumber, very offended.

"Sorry," you reply. And then you finally see it: the round door of a dome covered in coral.

-> 50 : Go up to the door

== 50
The station door is made of bronze, round like a giant porthole. In the middle, a huge lock. The handle won't move a millimetre.

A thick cable enters the dome from the side, like a root.

-> 51 [key] : Try the brass key
-> 53 [Octave] : Ask Octave for help
-> 52 [chart] : Look at Gaston's chart
-> 54 : Push with all your might

== 51
The key slides into the lock. You turn it with both legs. It resists... then, with a deep "clonk", the door swings open.

A big air bubble escapes and rushes up to the surface.

-> 56 : Enter the station

== 52
On the chart, a cross marks a "chimney" on the roof of the dome. You swim up: there is indeed a pipe, just wide enough for a small cicada.

You fold your wings, squeeze in... and land inside the station.

-> 56 : Look around

== 53
"A lock?" Octave smiles. "Octopuses have no bones: I get in everywhere."

He slips under the door through a tiny gap, like toothpaste. Click, clack: arms are busy inside.

The door opens. Octave waves at you with four arms.

-> 56 : Enter the station

== 54
You push. You pull. You push again. The door doesn't move, but a family of rainbow wrasse comes to watch, as if at a show.

"You need a key," says Hippolyta over the radio. "Or someone very bendy."

-> 22 : Look for the octopus
-> 55 : Give up and go back up

== 55 FIN perdu
You go back up with pictures of the door, the plate and the wrasse. That's already a fine discovery.

But nobody gets into the station this year. Hippolyta is already planning an expedition for next summer.

Moral: you go around a locked door, you don't push it.

== 56
Surprise: the station is full of air! A bubble trapped for a hundred years. You take off your helmet.

Copper and brass everywhere: a Morse key, coils, a big box where the cable comes in. On a desk lie a leather logbook and a paper tape.

Hippolyta, on the radio: "If you can reconnect the cable, we'll plug in the hydrophone!"

-> 57 : Read the paper tape
-> 58 : Open the big box
-> 59 : Leaf through the logbook

== 57
On the tape, the last message the station received:

-- .. .-. .- -.-. .-.. .

On the wall, an old half-faded chart:

A .-  C -.-.  E .  G --.
I ..  L .-..  M --  R .-.

Below, in ink: "The word on the tape opens the box."

-> 60 : MIRACLE
-> 61 : RECLAIM
-> 61 : MAGICAL

== 58
The big box is locked with a letter dial, like an old safe. A plate says: "PASSWORD".

You try 1234. Nothing. 0000? Nothing. PASSWORD? A spring goes "boing", offended.

-> 57 : Look for a clue in the room

== 59
The logbook is written in purple ink. The telegraph operators noted everything: the weather, the messages... and biscuit recipes!

The last page is from 1920: "Storm. We are going up. May the station keep watch."

It's a treasure of history.

-> 63 : Go up with the logbook
-> 57 : First, read the tape

== 60
M, I, R, A, C, L, E. A miracle indeed! You turn the dial letter by letter.

Clack! The box opens. Inside, copper contacts well protected by grease. You plug in the hydrophone wire that Hippolyta passed through the airlock.

Relays clatter, a small lamp lights up. A hundred years later, the station wakes up!

-> 62 : Listen

== 61
You turn the dial. The box goes "brrrt" and stays shut.

"Count the dots and dashes again," says Hippolyta over the radio. "A dot is short, a dash is long, and each group is one letter."

-> 57 : Read the tape again

== 62
In the headphones, the sea sings. The clicks of the dolphins, the crackle of the shrimps, the distant breath of a fin whale passing far away.

"It works!" shouts Hippolyta. "The sound goes down the cable all the way to the coast!"

The station has become an ear under the sea. All that's left is to tell the harbour.

-> 64 : Go back up to the surface

== 63 FIN gagne
You go back up with the logbook, safe in a waterproof bag.

In La Ciotat, the museum puts it in a glass case: "Logbook of the cable station, 1871-1920. Found by Cicadina."

The station still sleeps on the seabed, but its story is saved. And the museum throws a biscuit party, using the operators' recipe.

Another ending, a louder one, may be waiting for you...

== 64
The Little Nautilus surfaces. The storm is over, and the sun sets behind the Eagle's Beak cliff.

But the submarine's radio got wet. How can you tell the harbour that the station works?

"With light signals!" says Hippolyta. "The harbour expects a very simple message: OK."

"Partner! I've been looking everywhere for you! So, the gold? Where's the gold?"

== 67 FIN gagne
On the quay, a light answers: --- -.- . OK!

The next day, all of La Ciotat listens to the sea through the harbour loudspeakers: the dolphins, the shrimps, the whale. School classes come to visit the new observatory.

An octopus moves into the station, the old grouper guards the passage, and the cable sings again, a hundred years later.

Well done, Cicadina: the best discovery is the one you share!

== 68 FIN perdu
Three dots, three dashes, three dots: SOS!

Ten minutes later, a lifeboat, two jet skis and a helicopter surround you. The rescuers are very nice... a little less so once they understand.

The station works, but what everyone remembers is the cicada who called the rescue services to say OK.

Moral: in Morse code, one wrong letter changes everything.

== 69

After a long silence, the harbour office replies: .... .- .... .-

H, A, H, A. Hippolyta bursts out laughing. The harbour master has a sense of humour.

== 70

His eye gets misty.

"It's... it's more beautiful than gold. I must tell every gull in the harbour!"
