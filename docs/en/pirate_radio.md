# Pirate radio: a cicada broadcasts in FM on 433 MHz

**Admin > Pirate radio** (Radio pirate): the cicada broadcasts sound in **FM** on 433 MHz, with the CC1101 radio of the badge. You
listen to it with a **Flipper Zero** (Sub-GHz > Read RAW, FM476 preset, sound on), a **Portapack** (HackRF), an
**SDR** (RTL-SDR + SDR#, GQRX, SDR++...) or **another cicada** ("Listen to pirate radio", Écouter la radio
pirate). Three sources: a melody generated by the badge ("Au clair de la lune", public domain, no SD card needed), a
**1 kHz tone** (for the measures) or a **WAV file** of the SD card (`MUSIQUE` folder).

*Version française : [radio_pirate.md](../fr/radio_pirate.md).*

> **Legal note.** The 433.05 - 434.79 MHz band is an ISM / SRD band (short range devices): in France and in Europe,
> transmitting there is free under conditions (effective radiated power ≤ 10 mW, duty cycle limited depending on the
> use). It is **not** a broadcasting band: keep the Pirate radio for **short tests**, at **low power** (-10 dBm by
> default), in the room, without disturbing the other users of the band (remote controls, sensors...). This is why
> it is only available **from the admin mode**, and the transmission stops by itself after **10 minutes**.

## How it works

The CC1101 cannot do analog FM: it does digital modulation (2-FSK, GFSK, OOK...). The trick:

1. The CC1101 is in **2-FSK, asynchronous serial mode** (`PKTCTRL0 = 0x32`): it sends *f0 + deviation* when its GDO0
   pin is high, *f0 - deviation* when it is low, and it reads GDO0 **8 times per bit** of its programmed data rate
   (datasheet, "asynchronous serial operation"). The data rate is set to the maximum of 2-FSK, **500 kBaud**: GDO0 is
   therefore sampled at **4 MHz** (125 reads per period of the PWM).
2. The RP2040 drives GDO0 with a **PWM** of ~**32 kHz** (clk_sys / 3906 = 32,002 Hz at 125 MHz,
   `AUDIO_RADIO_WRAP` = 3905) whose **duty cycle follows the samples of the sound**: 50 % = silence, 0 % and 100 % =
   the peaks. A DMA channel, paced by a DMA timer at the sample rate, copies the samples into the register of the
   PWM, exactly as for the speaker (`audio.c`, output `AUDIO_OUT_RADIO`): no computation by the processor during the
   transmission. The **sound gain** (x1, x2, x4, `audio_set_radio_gain()`) amplifies the samples around the silence,
   clipped at the peaks.
3. An NFM receiver has a narrow channel filter (~12.5 kHz): it does not see the switching at 32 kHz but its
   **average**, *f0 + deviation × (2 × duty cycle - 1)*: the instantaneous frequency follows the sound, that is
   **FM**. The products of the PWM fall at ±32 kHz from the carrier (and their multiples): outside a 12.5 kHz NFM
   channel. A WFM receiver, wider, demodulates them too, but at 32 kHz, above the audio: its audio filter removes
   them.
4. A receiver that **slices** the frequency (above or below the centre: the listening cicada, a Flipper Zero in Read
   RAW) follows the PWM itself: the duty cycle of the received bit stream **is** the sound. For it to be at full
   scale, the deviation must be about the one the receiver expects (47.6 kHz for the FM476 preset of the Flipper).
   The 32 kHz PWM makes **2 periods per sample** of the listening cicada (16 kHz): no beat between the PWM and the
   measure.

The network of the cicadas is paused during the transmission (`net_pause()`), the radio is borrowed from
`radio_tools.c` (`radio_tools_claim()` / `radio_tools_release()`) then given back to the network, which configures
it again (GFSK profile, sync word, reception). The transmission stops at the end of the track, with the left or right
wing, when the page is left, and after 10 minutes in any case.

## The settings

The page shows, one per row: **Source**, **Frequency** (Fréquence), **Power** (Puissance), **Deviation** (Excursion),
**Sound gain** (Gain du son), **Speaker** (Haut-parleur), then **> Transmit** (> Émettre; **> Choose the file**, in
French > Choisir le fichier, with the SD file source), and at the bottom "ISM 433 MHz: short tests" or the
message of the last stop.

Sides: previous / next row; wings: previous / next value; on the last row, right wing: transmit (or choose the
file), left wing: back. Long press on the left wing: quit.

| Setting | Values | Default |
|---|---|---|
| Source | Melody (Mélodie), 1 kHz tone (Tonalité 1 kHz), SD file (Fichier SD) | Melody |
| Frequency | 433.300 / 433.650 / **433.920** / 434.200 / 434.500 MHz | 433.920 MHz |
| Power | -20, **-10**, 0, +5, +10 dBm | -10 dBm |
| Deviation | 2.5 / 5 / 12.5 / 25 / **47.6 kHz** (peak, sound at full scale); 47.6 kHz shows "(Flipper)" | 47.6 kHz |
| Sound gain | x1 / **x2** / x4: the samples amplified around the silence, clipped | x2 |
| Speaker | yes / no: the badge plays the sound too | no |

- **Deviation**: **47.6 kHz** = the **FM476** preset of the Flipper Zero, and the best sound on the listening cicada;
  **5 kHz** (or 2.5 kHz) for a narrow band FM receiver (walkie-talkie, Portapack or SDR in NFM); 12.5 and 25 kHz in
  between (wide NFM, WFM).
- **Sound gain**: WAV files are often recorded low; x2 or x4 raises them (a sound already loud is clipped, hence
  distorted: go back to x1).
- **Melody**: "Au clair de la lune" (~23 s), sine with a small envelope, 16 kHz.
- **1 kHz tone**: continuous sine at 78 % of the full scale before the gain (until stopped or the 10 minutes):
  for the measures.
- **SD file**: the `.WAV` files of the card: it starts in the `MUSIQUE` folder (or the root) and browses the
  folders and sub-folders (shown "Name/" first in the list; right wing: open the folder or transmit the file, left
  wing: the parent folder, then back to the settings from the root). The files are read by the WAV player of the badge (8,
  16, 24 or 32 bits, float, mono or stereo, up to 192 kHz). Recommended: mono 16 kHz, normalized (`audio2wav.py`).
  The sound is sent as is (no pre-emphasis, no filter): a very high-pitched sound widens the spectrum a little.

Radio parameters: asynchronous 2-FSK, data rate 500 kBaud (`MDMCFG4/3` = `0x4E` / `0x3B` at 26 MHz), `DEVIATN`
computed for the measured crystal (at 26 MHz: `0x05` = 2.58 kHz, `0x15` = 5.16 kHz, `0x30` = 12.70 kHz, `0x40` =
25.39 kHz, `0x47` = 47.61 kHz), `FREND0 = 0x10` (PATABLE[0]), PATABLE `0x0E` / `0x34` / `0x60` / `0x84` / `0xC0`,
the frequency correction of the radio tuning (`FSCTRL0`, see Settings > Radio tuning) is kept.

During the transmission, the page shows **ON AIR** (ÉMISSION) in large, the frequency, the power and the deviation,
the source and the elapsed time.

## Listening

### With a Flipper Zero

Sub-GHz > **Read RAW**, then Config: frequency **433.92 MHz** (or the one chosen), modulation **FM476**, **Sound**
on; on the cicada, the deviation **47.6 kHz (Flipper)** (the default). Start the recording (REC): the Flipper plays
what it receives on its speaker.

Why it works: in Read RAW, the CC1101 of the Flipper slices the frequency (like the listening cicada) and its speaker
gets the result, i.e. the PWM of the transmitter, whose duty cycle is the sound. With the former setting (±5 kHz
deviation, 122 kHz PWM), the reception was good but the sound almost inaudible: the variation of the duty cycle was
tiny. With 47.6 kHz of deviation and a 32 kHz PWM, the sound arrives at full scale. This gain of level was measured
with the listening cicada (see [Results](#results)), not yet checked by ear on the Flipper.

Sub-GHz > **Frequency Analyzer** also shows the carrier (frequency and RSSI) when the cicada transmits.

### With a Portapack (HackRF + Mayhem)

**Audio** application (Receive > Audio), frequency **433.920 MHz** (or the one chosen), medium LNA/VGA gains,
squelch at 0 to begin with:
- deviation **5 kHz** on the cicada: modulation **NFM**, filter **16k** (or **11k** for 2.5 kHz);
- deviation **47.6 kHz**: modulation **WFM** (a narrow NFM would be overdriven and distorted).

Come close: at -10 dBm the range is a few metres.

### With an SDR (RTL-SDR, Airspy, HackRF...)

SDR#, GQRX, SDR++, centred on the frequency chosen: in **NFM** (~12.5 kHz, 16 kHz wide) with **5 kHz** of deviation
on the cicada; with **47.6 kHz**, in **WFM** or a wide NFM (≥ 100 kHz of bandwidth). On the waterfall, you see the
carrier and, on each side, the lines of the PWM at ±32 kHz (and their multiples).

### With another cicada: "Listen to pirate radio"

The second badge is a real receiver for this signal:

1. Its CC1101 is in **asynchronous 2-FSK reception** on the same frequency, with a **wide channel of 406 kHz** (the
   PWM of the transmitter, at 32 kHz, and the deviation up to 47.6 kHz go through it) and the same data rate of
   500 kBaud. Its demodulator outputs on **GDO2** a 1 when the frequency received is above the centre, a 0 below
   (`IOCFG2 = 0x0D`): this bit stream follows the PWM of the transmitter, its **duty cycle is the sound** (with a
   slope of about one half).
2. GDO2 is the "B" pin of a PWM slice of the RP2040: in `PWM_DIV_B_HIGH` mode, its counter counts the cycles of the
   clock (125 MHz) **while GDO2 is high**. A DMA channel paced at **16 kHz** copies this counter into a ring: the
   difference between two copies is the time high over 62.5 µs (exactly 2 periods of the PWM of the transmitter),
   exact to the cycle, without the processor.
3. The main loop turns it into sound (duty cycle minus its average), plays it on the speaker and measures it every
   second: frequency by counting the zero crossings, purity of 1 kHz with the Goertzel algorithm, level (amplitude
   in % of the half range of the duty cycle), RSSI.
4. **Frequency correction (AFC)**: the crystals of the two badges differ (up to ±20 ppm each, i.e. ±17 kHz).
   The average duty cycle must be 50 %: otherwise the receiver shifts its frequency in steps of ~400 Hz (every
   250 ms, in steps of 2 kHz when the signal is saturated), only when a carrier is received (RSSI ≥ -92 dBm). If it
   reaches ±32 kHz without finding it, it tries the reverse polarity of the demodulator.

Screen: frequency, signal (dBm and gauge), frequency correction, tone detected (only when it is a real tone:
purity ≥ 50 % and level ≥ 5 %), level and average duty cycle, state of the squelch. Sides: previous / next channel;
right wing: squelch on / off ("Squelch: on", Silencieux); left wing: back.

On the USB serial port, one line per second (example values):

```
pirate: rx tone 1000 Hz, level 38 %, purity 1 kHz 97 %, duty 50 %, rssi -62 dBm, afc +1191 Hz
```

## Test with two badges

1. Badge A (receiver): open "Listen to pirate radio" (Écouter la radio pirate), on 433.920 MHz. Plug its USB, open the serial port.
2. Badge B (transmitter, admin mode): Admin > Pirate radio, Source "1 kHz tone", Power **0 dBm** (or +10 dBm
   if the badges are far apart: at -10 dBm one measures ~-97 dBm at 1 m, at the edge of the sensitivity in a 406 kHz
   channel), Deviation 47.6 kHz and Sound gain x2 (the defaults), "> Transmit". The two badges 1 m apart.
3. On A, within a few seconds: RSSI clearly above -92 dBm, the correction settles, and the serial port prints
   `rx tone 1000 Hz` (±5 Hz) with a high purity; the speaker whistles at 1 kHz.
4. Then the Melody (you recognize it on A), then a WAV of the SD card. Stop: left wing on B; the line `no carrier`
   shows up on A.

### Results

Measured on the listening cicada, 1 kHz test tone, same badges, same distance (on a desk):

| Transmitter | 0 dBm | +10 dBm |
|---|---|---|
| Before: 122 kHz PWM, 5 kHz deviation | purity 32 %, level 17 % | purity 84 %, level 25 %, tone ~1130 Hz (zero crossings) |
| Now: 32 kHz PWM, 47.6 kHz deviation, gain x2 | purity 81 %, level 55 % | tone 1000-1001 Hz, purity 95 %, level 95 % |

## Messages on the serial port

- `pirate: start on 433920000 Hz, deviation 47607 Hz (DEVIATN for 47600 Hz), power -10 dBm (PATABLE 0x34),
  source Mélodie, PWM 32002 Hz, data rate 500000`
- `pirate: stop (fin du morceau | arrêt manuel | page quittée | sécurité : 10 min max) after N s` (end of the track,
  manual stop, page left, safety: 10 min max)
- `pirate: error, ...`, `pirate: radio busy ...` (OOK receiver active, the talk badge for example: try again
  elsewhere than on this badge).
- Receiver: `pirate: rx start ...`, `pirate: rx tone ...`, `pirate: rx afc at its limit, polarity -1`,
  `pirate: rx stop`.

## Limits

- The "cicada" receiver has a wide channel (406 kHz): it is less sensitive than a real NFM receiver, and more
  sensitive to the other transmitters of the band. The sound received is correct but a little compressed.
- With 47.6 kHz of deviation, the signal takes about 160 kHz (Carson's rule: 2 × (47.6 + 32) kHz): it stays inside
  the 433.05 - 434.79 MHz band on the 5 channels offered, but it is no longer "narrow band". For an NFM receiver,
  choose 5 kHz.
- No pre-emphasis nor filtering of the sound when transmitting; the x2 / x4 gain clips the sounds already loud.
- The real frequency depends on the crystal of the badge (see Settings > Radio tuning): a few kHz of offset are
  normal, the Portapack tolerates them in NFM 16k.
